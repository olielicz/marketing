# Cold Outreach Playbook — OliSalesTrack (E-commerce Sellers)

**Primary channel:** direct outreach + community posts — e-commerce sellers don't browse SaaS discovery directories looking for a profit-tracking tool; they respond to a specific, relevant pitch that speaks to a number they don't currently know (their real margin after fees, refunds, and ad spend). Use this alongside the Tier 3 Reddit/community posts already listed in `directory-submission-list.md`.

---

## Step 1 — Build your list (free, manual, ~1-2 hrs for 25-50 contacts)

Sources for finding solo e-commerce sellers / small store owners (all free to browse):
- **Shopify Community forums** — active posters in the "Sales" and "Accounting & Taxes" boards are self-selecting for exactly this pain point
- **r/shopify, r/ecommerce, r/FulfillmentByAmazon** — identify sellers who post about margins, refunds, or "which numbers should I track" questions, then reach out respectfully (don't cold-DM strangers who haven't engaged with the topic)
- **Etsy/Shopify store "About" or "Contact" pages** — many small store owners list a direct business email
- **LinkedIn** — search "Shopify store owner" or "e-commerce founder" + niche; many list an email in their About section or accept LinkedIn messages
- **Indie Hackers** — sellers posting revenue updates are already comfortable talking numbers publicly, which makes them a warm audience for a margin-tracking pitch

**Keep this targeted:** solo sellers and small stores (1-5 person teams, not enterprise brands with an in-house finance team) are the actual buyer — they feel "I don't actually know my real profit margin after fees and refunds" directly, and don't have a BI team already solving it for them.

Track in a simple spreadsheet: Name, Store/Platform (Shopify/Amazon/Etsy/etc.), Email/contact method, Date contacted, Reply Y/N, Follow-up date.

## Step 2 — Email templates

**Subject line options:**
- "Quick question about your store's real margin"
- "[First name] — do you actually know your true profit per order?"

**Body:**

Most sellers I talk to know their revenue number cold but couldn't tell you their actual margin without an hour in a spreadsheet. OliSalesTrack does that math automatically and flags patterns (like which SKU is quietly losing you money on refunds).

Worth a quick look? Happy to answer anything directly.

Best,
[Your name]

## Step 3 — Follow-up sequence (only for non-responders, send once, 5-7 days later)

**Subject line:** "Following up — quick link"

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

Just floating this back up in case it got buried — here's that OliSalesTrack link again: [link]

No pressure either way.

[Your name]

**Do not send more than one follow-up.** A single polite follow-up is normal; repeated follow-ups after silence reads as spam and damages your sender reputation for future outreach.

---

## Step 4 — Daily execution cadence

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Build list of 25-30 contacts. Send Template A to first 15. |
| 2 | Send Template A/B (split test) to next 15. |
| 3-5 | Continue to reach 50 total. Begin Step 3 follow-ups to Day 1's non-responders (5-7 day mark). |

**Second round:** repeat with a fresh batch of 25 contacts, using whichever template/subject line got the best reply rate the first time.

---

## Sending mechanics (free tools)

- **Volume:** 15-20 emails/day from a normal Gmail/Google Workspace account stays well within free sending limits and avoids spam-filter flags that bulk-sending tools can trigger
- **Personalization:** use the actual first name and store name/platform — generic mail-merge blasts get lower reply rates and are easier to flag as spam
- **Tracking replies:** a simple spreadsheet is enough at this volume; no paid CRM needed for 50-100 contacts

## What counts as a "win" at this stage

- **Reply rate** is the metric that matters most, not opens. A 5-10% reply rate on cold outreach to a well-targeted list is a reasonable, honest benchmark for a new product with no existing reputation — don't expect higher on the first attempt.
- Even a "not for me, but try [other seller]" reply is valuable — always ask for a referral in your reply if someone declines politely.
- Watch for sellers who reply with "how is this different from Baremetrics/QuickBooks" — that's a real buying signal, not a rejection; answer it directly (OliSalesTrack is flat-rate and built for sellers who find Baremetrics/QuickBooks overkill or too expensive for a single store).
