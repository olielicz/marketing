# Cold Outreach Playbook — Oli-Locator (Home-Improvement Contractors)

**Why cold outreach instead of Product Hunt/directories:** Solo tradespeople and small
home-services businesses don't browse SaaS discovery platforms looking for tools — they
respond to direct, relevant outreach and referrals. This is a relationship-driven buying
motion, not a discovery-driven one. Per the master calendar, this starts Week 3, Day 17,
targeting 100 contractors by end of week, then a second batch of 50 in Week 4.

---

## Step 1 — Build your list (free, manual, ~1-2 hrs for 50-100 contacts)

Sources for finding solo tradespeople/small home-services businesses (all free to browse):
- **Google Business Profile / Google Maps** — search "[trade] near [city]" (e.g. "house
  cleaning near Austin", "pest control near Manchester") — most listings show a phone
  number, website, or a "message" option
- **Nextdoor business listings** — local trade businesses often list contact info publicly
- **Trade association directories** — many regional/national trade bodies (cleaning,
  roofing, HVAC, etc.) have public, searchable member directories
- **Facebook Business Pages** — many solo contractors run a Facebook page with a public
  "Message" button or listed email — legitimate direct outreach to a business's own listed
  contact channel, not scraped/purchased data
- **Local trade-specific Facebook Groups** — many small trade businesses post in local
  "recommend a tradesperson" groups; their own posts/replies often include contact info

**Keep this ethical and effective:** target solo operators and small businesses (1-5
people), not large national franchises — they're the actual buyer for a tool like this,
and they're far more likely to reply to a personal, non-corporate email.

Track in a simple spreadsheet: Name, Trade, City, Email/Contact method, Date contacted,
Reply Y/N, Follow-up date.

## Step 2 — Email templates

### Template A — Cold intro (first contact)

**Subject line options:**
- "Quick question about how you're finding new jobs"
- "[First name] — a tool for [trade] businesses in [city]"
- "Saw your [trade] business in [city] — quick question"

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

I noticed you run a [trade] business in [city] — I built a tool called Oli-Locator
specifically for solo pros and small trade businesses like yours who are tired of paying
$15-$85+ every single time a lead comes in on platforms like Angi or Thumbtack.

Most contractors I talk to are either overpaying per-lead as they get busier, or juggling
a separate lead source, a separate way to track calls, and a separate inbox for
conversations. Oli-Locator puts leads, a call center, and a shared inbox in one flat-rate
subscription — no per-lead fees, ever.

There's a free demo account you can try right now, no signup needed: [demo login link]

Worth a 10-minute look? Happy to answer any questions directly, or you can just poke
around the demo yourself.

Best,
[Your name]

---

### Template B — Shorter/more casual variant (A/B test this against Template A)

**Subject line:** "Built this for [trade] pros like you — 2 min look?"

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

I built a tool for solo tradespeople and small home-services businesses — leads, a shared
inbox, and call tracking, all in one flat-rate app instead of paying per lead.

Free demo, no signup: [demo login link]

If it's not useful, no worries at all — just thought it might save you some money and
time. Happy to answer anything if you have questions.

[Your name]

---

## Step 3 — Follow-up sequence (only for non-responders, send once, 5-7 days later)

**Subject line:** "Following up — quick demo link"

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

Just floating this back up in case it got buried — here's that free demo link for
Oli-Locator again: [demo login link]

No pressure either way, just wanted to make sure it didn't get lost.

[Your name]

**Do not send more than one follow-up.** A single polite follow-up is normal and expected;
repeated follow-ups after silence reads as spam and damages your sender reputation for
future outreach.

---

## Step 4 — Daily execution cadence (Week 3, Days 17-21)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 17 | Build list of 40-50 contacts. Send Template A to first 20. |
| 18 | Send Template A/B (split test) to next 20. |
| 19 | Send remaining contacts to reach 100 total for the week. Begin sending Step 3 follow-ups to Day 17's non-responders (5-7 day mark starts after this week). |
| 20-21 | Continue outreach cadence, log replies, note which subject line/template performed better. |

**Week 4 (Day 27):** repeat with a fresh batch of 50 contacts, using whichever
template/subject line got the best reply rate in Week 3.

---

## Sending mechanics (free tools)

- **Volume:** 20 emails/day from a normal Gmail/Google Workspace account stays well within
  free sending limits and avoids spam-filter flags that bulk-sending tools can trigger
- **Personalization:** always use the actual first name, trade, and city/business name —
  generic mail-merge blasts get lower reply rates and are easier to flag as spam
- **Tracking replies:** a simple spreadsheet is enough at this volume; no paid CRM needed
  for 100-150 contacts/month

## What counts as a "win" at this stage

- **Reply rate** is the metric that matters most in Week 3's checkpoint, not opens. A
  5-10% reply rate on cold outreach to a well-targeted list is a reasonable, honest
  benchmark for a new product with no existing reputation — don't expect higher on the
  first attempt.
- Even a "not interested, but here's someone who might want this" reply is valuable —
  always ask for a referral in your reply if someone declines politely.
