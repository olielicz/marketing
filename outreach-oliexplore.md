# Cold Outreach Playbook — OliExplore (Social Media Managers / Agencies)

**Primary channel:** direct outreach + community posts — social media managers respond better to a specific, relevant pitch than to browsing SaaS discovery directories. Use this alongside (not instead of) the Tier 3 Reddit/community posts already listed in `directory-submission-list.md`.

---

## Step 1 — Build your list (free, manual, ~1-2 hrs for 25-50 contacts)

Sources for finding solo social media managers / small agencies (all free to browse):
- **LinkedIn** — search "social media manager" or "social media agency" + your target city/niche; many list an email in their About section or accept LinkedIn messages
- **Agency "our team" / "contact" pages** — small social-media-focused agencies often list a direct contact email
- **Instagram/X bios** — creators and freelance social managers frequently list a business email in their bio
- **r/socialmedia, r/marketing** — identify active, helpful posters who mention managing multiple accounts, then reach out respectfully (don't cold-DM strangers who haven't engaged with the topic)

**Keep this targeted:** solo social media managers and small agencies (1-10 client accounts) are the actual buyer — they feel the "reformat this post for 6 platforms" pain directly, unlike a large agency with a dedicated ops team.

Track in a simple spreadsheet: Name, Company/Freelance, Platforms they manage, Email/contact method, Date contacted, Reply Y/N, Follow-up date.

## Step 2 — Email templates

### Template A — Cold intro (first contact)

**Subject line options:**
- "Quick question about your posting workflow"
- "[First name] — a tool for social managers juggling multiple platforms"

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

I noticed you manage social for [company/clients] — I built a tool called OliExplore specifically for this: it collects your existing posts from Facebook, Instagram, X, TikTok and Threads, rewrites them into a catchier variant, and publishes the result to all your connected platforms (including LinkedIn) in one click.

Most social managers I talk to are manually reformatting the same winning post 4-5 times for different platforms. OliExplore automates that whole loop.

Starts at $27/mo with a 14-day free trial — [link]

Worth a quick look? Happy to answer anything directly.

Best,
[Your name]

### Template B — Shorter/more casual variant (A/B test against Template A)

**Subject line:** "Built this so you stop reformatting the same post 5 times"

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

Built a tool that collects your best posts, rewrites them into a catchier version, and publishes to every platform you're on — instead of you manually redoing the same post for each app.

14-day free trial, from $27/mo: [link]

[Your name]

## Step 3 — Follow-up sequence (only for non-responders, send once, 5-7 days later)

**Subject line:** "Following up — quick link"

**Body:**

Hi [First Name],

Just floating this back up in case it got buried — here's that OliExplore link again: [link]

No pressure either way.

[Your name]

**Do not send more than one follow-up.** A single polite follow-up is normal; repeated follow-ups after silence reads as spam.

---

## Step 4 — Daily execution cadence

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Build list of 25-30 contacts. Send Template A to first 15. |
| 2 | Send Template A/B (split test) to next 15. |
| 3-5 | Continue to reach 50 total. Begin Step 3 follow-ups to Day 1's non-responders (5-7 day mark). |

**Second round:** repeat with a fresh batch of 25 contacts, using whichever template/subject line got the best reply rate the first time.

---

## Sending mechanics (free tools)

- **Volume:** 15-20 emails/day from a normal Gmail/Google Workspace account stays well within free sending limits
- **Personalization:** use the actual first name and company/platform they manage — generic blasts get lower reply rates
- **Tracking replies:** a simple spreadsheet is enough at this volume

## What counts as a "win" at this stage

- Reply rate matters more than opens. A 5-10% reply rate on a well-targeted list is a reasonable benchmark for a new product with no existing reputation.
- Even a "not for me, but try [other person]" reply is valuable — always ask for a referral if someone declines politely.
